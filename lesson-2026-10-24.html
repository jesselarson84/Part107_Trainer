Your Remote Pilot Certificate

Who can get one, how it's issued, and what keeps it valid once it's in your wallet.

Sat, Oct 24~5 min read3 quiz questions

Who can get one01

To be eligible (§107.61), you must:

  • Be at least 16 years old.
  • Read, speak, write, and understand English. If a medical reason prevents one of these, the FAA can add operating limits instead of denying you.
  • Not know of any physical or mental condition that would make flying unsafe. There's no medical exam.
  • Pass the initial knowledge test (or, if you already hold a Part 61 pilot certificate with a current flight review, take the FAA's online training instead).

Failed the test? You have to wait 14 calendar days before you can take it again (§107.71).

Test trap"You need an FAA medical certificate." You don't. You just can't fly if you know or have reason to know of a condition that would make it unsafe.

From passing to holding the card02

After you pass, you apply in IACRA using the 17-digit exam ID from your test report. Results can take up to 48 hours to show up there.

1ApplySubmit your application in IACRA with your knowledge test results.
2TSA checkA security background check. Once it clears, you can print a temporary certificate.
3Permanent cardMailed to you. The temporary one is good for up to 120 days.

The temporary certificate counts. You can fly Part 107 with it until the permanent card arrives.

Staying current: 24 calendar months03

The card itself doesn't expire. What lapses is your knowledge recency: to fly as remote pilot in command, you must have passed the initial test or completed recurrent training within the previous 24 calendar months (§107.65).

Calendar months run to the end of the month, not to the same day.

How to renew: the FAA's free online recurrent course (ALC-677) on FAASafety.gov. No testing center needed.

Test trapOlder guides say you retake a "recurrent knowledge test" at a testing center. That was replaced by free online training on April 6, 2021.

Keeping it valid04

  • Carry it: have your certificate and a photo ID on you whenever you fly as remote PIC (§107.7).
  • Moved? Update your address with the FAA within 30 days, or you can't use the certificate after that (§107.77).
  • New name? Apply with your certificate plus a marriage license, court order, or similar document.
  • No certificate? Someone can still fly the controls if they're directly supervised by a remote PIC who can immediately take over (§107.12).
  • Drug convictions or refusing an alcohol test can mean denial of your application for up to 1 year, or suspension or revocation of your certificate (§107.57, §107.59).
Test trapDon't mix up the numbers: 14 days to update your drone's registration, 30 days to update your certificate address.
