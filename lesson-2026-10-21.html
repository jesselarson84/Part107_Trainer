Day 3 · Rules & Regulations

Right of Way & Prohibited Operations

Who gives way in the sky, what you can never do with a drone, flying from a moving car or boat, and why it's one pilot, one drone.

Wed, Oct 21~5 min read3 quiz questions

You always give way01

The rule: your drone must yield the right of way to all aircraft, airborne vehicles, and launch and reentry vehicles. Planes, helicopters, balloons, gliders, even a rocket.

Yielding means: give way, and don't pass over, under, or ahead of it unless you're well clear.

Also: never fly so close to another aircraft that you create a collision hazard.

Test trapAnswers saying the manned pilot should avoid you, or that you can pass "just above" or "just below" it. The drone yields, every time.

Never allowed02

  • Careless or reckless flying that endangers someone's life or property.
  • Dropping an object in a way that creates an undue hazard to people or property. Dropping something safely isn't banned outright.
  • Carrying hazardous material (as defined by DOT rules in 49 CFR 171.8).

No waiver: none of these three can be waived.

Flying from a moving vehicle03

Neverfrom a moving aircraft
Maybefrom a moving car or boat
Waivablewith an FAA waiver

From a moving car, truck, or boat, you may fly only if both are true:

  • The drone is over a sparsely populated area.
  • It's not carrying someone else's property for pay.
Test trap"Never from a moving vehicle." Too strict. A moving aircraft is never OK, but a moving car or boat can be, over a sparsely populated area.

One drone at a time & visual observers04

One at a time: a person can't be the RPIC, work the controls, or be the visual observer for more than one drone at the same time.

Visual observer (VO): optional. A person the RPIC picks to help see and avoid other traffic and obstacles.

If you use one, the RPIC, the person on the controls, and the VO must:

  • Keep effective communication at all times.
  • Scan the airspace for collision hazards together.
  • Track where the drone is by direct sight.

Which of today's rules can be waived?05

A waiver is FAA permission to skip a specific rule. Today's rules split like this:

✓WaivableMoving vehicle, one drone at a time, and yielding right of way.
✗Not waivableCareless or reckless flying, dropping objects unsafely, and hazmat.
!Also noFlying close enough to create a collision hazard.
