Day 2 · Rules & Regulations

Pilot Duties & Reporting

Who's in charge of a flight, what you must check first, when you're grounded, and when the FAA needs to hear about a mishap.

Tue, Oct 20~5 min read3 quiz questions

Remote pilot in command01

Every flight has one remote pilot in command (RPIC), named before or during the flight.

Final authority: the RPIC is directly responsible for the flight and has the last word on it. Not the drone's owner, not the client.

Letting someone else fly: a person without a certificate may work the controls only under the RPIC's direct supervision, and only if the RPIC can immediately take control.

Test trapAnswers that hand final authority to the owner, the client, or the person on the sticks. It's always the RPIC.

Before every flight02

The RPIC must assess the operating environment, looking at risks to people and property on the ground and in the air.

Condition for safe operation: check the drone before each flight. If it stops being safe mid-flight, you can't keep flying it.

Alcohol & drugs03

Part 107 borrows the manned-aircraft rule. You can't fly as RPIC, work the controls, or act as a visual observer:

8 hrsafter any alcoholic drink
0.04alcohol concentration or higher
Impairedby alcohol or any drug

All three apply at once. Waiting 8 hours doesn't help if you're still at 0.04 or still feel it.

Medical condition: you also can't fly if you know (or should know) a physical or mental condition would get in the way of flying safely.

How: file it online through FAADroneZone, or contact your local FAA Flight Standards District Office.

Test trapSwapped numbers like "10 days and $1,000" or "30 days and $500." It's 10 calendar days and more than $500.

Carry it and show it05

When you fly under Part 107, have your remote pilot certificate and a photo ID (like a driver's license) on you. Show both if any of these ask:

  • The FAA
  • The NTSB (National Transportation Safety Board)
  • The TSA (Transportation Security Administration)
  • Any federal, state, or local law enforcement officer

On request, you must also let the FAA inspect or test your drone and show any records the rules require you to keep.

Test trap"Only the FAA can ask for it." Wrong. A local police officer can ask too.
